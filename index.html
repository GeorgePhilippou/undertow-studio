WELCOME TO UNDERTOW

Start with a song.

Choose song opens the library. Start with Hear the loop, then use the layer switches below the pads to build your backing. Alone lets you hear one layer; tap it again to return to your mix. Choose a named passage or the extended study and slow it to 50% or 75%.

Follow along shows a moving marker and lights each pad when its note arrives. The width of each note on the timing strip shows its length. Play it yourself removes the programmed instrument and hides the hints so you can try the passage from memory. Tap Show hints to see the guide again. This is a learning aid, not an automatic timing grade. Quiet studies enable a click. Play, Stop and Speed stay together in the transport bar. Edit sound & instrument opens the sound controls. How to shape this sound explains Tone and Space and can restore the study’s sound settings. Expand Edit the beat for the drum grid, twelve beat starters, kits and fills. More tools opens advanced lessons, sampling, saving and export.

Press Play to hear a starting point. Choose a song study from the tape archive, then make it yours. The fifteen song tapes are simplified, synthesized learning arrangements; the three original sketches are starting points of our own.

  1. Write a pattern. Tap cells to add or remove hits. Drag to paint. Switch between pattern pages; playback follows automatically. The studies now span longer passages. Idioteque uses fifteen bars so its drum and chord cycles realign. Each page shows sixteen steps; scroll the page buttons for longer patterns.
  2. Find a melody. Open Bass or Keys and draw notes. Each tape has its own pitch palette and instrument. Some Keys rows trigger labeled chord voicings; others play single notes.
  3. Learn a whole arrangement. Idioteque and Everything in Its Right Place have six guided exercises, adjustable practice speed, click, count-in, playable chord voicings, section loops, and a full instrumental teaching arrangement. These teach the parts and structure; they are not recording-synchronized full transcriptions.
  4. Compare. Load your own recording in the reference deck, choose A and B times, and slow it down. Reference files stay local and are not included in project exports.
  5. Take it apart. Solo Keys to hear the main part. Rebuild clears it and shows dotted reference notes; tap those cells to reconstruct the phrase. Restore track reloads just the selected instrument.
  6. Sample anything. Import an audio file, trim its start and end, and play four equal slices with the pads. Write slices into the Sample track.
  7. Perform. Move across the effects pad to change filter and echo. Pencil pressure sets note velocity on supported devices; fingers and mice work too.
  8. Keep it. Sessions autosave here. Save project downloads a portable file including imported audio. Export WAV renders two practice-loop repeats, or the entire teaching arrangement when full arrangement mode is selected, plus an effects tail.

Space = play / pause · 1–4 = instrument pads · ⌘/Ctrl Z = undo.
iPad: open in Safari and tap Play to enable audio. Squeeze, barrel rotation, and double-tap require a native app and aren’t used here. Keep the app in the foreground during playback.
